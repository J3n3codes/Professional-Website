Biomedical Engineering and mathematics

Detailed mathematics and statistics syllabus

Real Analysis was not covered under coursework. Other core CS courses such as Data Structures, Analysis of Algorithms, and Operating Systems were not covered in the listed coursework.

Linear Algebra — Applied Mathematics 4
  • n-dimensional vector spaces; metric spaces; norms and inner products; orthogonality; Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization.
  • Matrix theory; eigenvalues and eigenvectors; Cayley–Hamilton theorem; similarity and diagonalization; functions of square matrices.
  • Derogatory and non-derogatory matrices; quadratic forms and canonical forms; rank, index, and signature; Sylvester's law of inertia; definiteness.
  • Singular value decomposition (SVD).
Calculus — Applied Mathematics 1
  • Complex numbers and De Moivre's theorem.
  • Matrix types, rank, PAQ form, systems of linear equations, Gauss and Gauss–Jordan methods, Crout LU decomposition, Gauss–Seidel and Jacobi methods, and Scilab.
  • Higher derivatives and Leibniz theorem; partial derivatives and Euler's theorem.
  • Applications of partial differentiation, extrema, Lagrange multipliers, Jacobians, Taylor and Maclaurin series, indeterminate forms, and L'Hôpital's rule.
  • Least-squares curve fitting, regression, and Scilab.
Calculus — Applied Mathematics 2
  • Curve tracing and solid geometry; Beta and Gamma functions; differentiation under the integral sign; rectification.
  • Exact and linear ordinary differential equations; constant-coefficient equations; Cauchy–Euler homogeneous equations; Legendre equations; variation of parameters and engineering applications.
  • Numerical ODE methods: Taylor, Euler, modified Euler, and fourth-order Runge–Kutta.
  • Double and multiple integrals in Cartesian, cylindrical, and spherical coordinates; area, mass, and volume applications.
  • Interpolation and Newton–Cotes methods; trapezoidal and Simpson rules; Scilab.
Calculus — Applied Mathematics 3 and 4
  • Differential and partial differentiation, maxima and minima, Jacobians, series, ordinary differential equations, multiple integrals, and numerical integration.
  • Laplace transforms, properties, inverse Laplace transforms, and applications to ODEs.
  • Calculus of variations: Euler–Lagrange equation, isoperimetric problems, higher derivatives, and Rayleigh–Ritz method.
  • Complex integration, Cauchy's theorem and formula, Taylor and Laurent series, zeros, singularities, poles, residues, and the residue theorem.
Probability, statistics, and biostatistics
  • Descriptive statistics; probability and Bayes' theorem; binomial, Poisson, and normal distributions.
  • Sampling distributions, estimation, confidence intervals, sample sizes, hypothesis tests, Type I and Type II errors, and statistical power.
  • ANOVA and study designs; regression and correlation; chi-square goodness-of-fit, independence, and homogeneity tests.
  • Graduate public-health biostatistics coursework included linear regression and modeling, categorical data analysis, causal inference, and longitudinal analysis of public-health data.
Programming and statistical computing
  • Structured Programming Approach / C.
  • Object Oriented Programming and Methodology.
  • R and Intermediate R; SAS.
  • Scilab was used in numerical methods coursework.

Course-specific syllabi for these programming courses were not included in the supplied material.
